merge: one sheet Jim rules three taste questions from, and the coverage filter dies

Charter 26, PASS on the sheet and an honest WEAK on question 1 - where the weakness IS the finding.

Three rows, one variable per question, every panel labelled with what it is and what it costs, no
companion document needed. Questions are phrased for the owner, not the gate: how much of the
neighbouring land do you want to see; a soft bloom or a disk with an edge; standing at the road end
looking up your land, how far off the axis.

Question 1 ships three legal options and none of them is the farmland Jim asked for, because the
lane measured that no farmland option EXISTS: a stubble surround sits 7 from a colour the parcel
itself draws, dry field 10, mown pasture 6, against the classifier's 10-point tolerance and today's
neutral tone at 16. Rather than manufacture a third look to fill the row, the lane made the absence
falsifiable - `no_warm_land_tone_can_be_told_from_the_parcel_it_would_surround` fails the day a warm
surround becomes separable. The lead's own eye confirms the row: the three options are nearly
indistinguishable, which is exactly what 2.6 luma of separation predicts.

The coverage filter is gone. `SKYLINE_COVERAGE_BOUND` had been used to DROP viewpoints before
asserting agreement; coverage is now a FAIL bound and agreement is asserted over every viewpoint.
Behaviour-preserving today - all five poses sit 0.197 to 0.380 above the bound, so nothing was being
filtered - but NOT assertion-preserving, and that is the point: the test previously proved only that
the viewpoints which happened to have coverage agreed, and could have passed on one of five. It now
proves every viewpoint carries enough skyline to compare AND that every one agrees.

Two things the lane did that are worth keeping as practice. It wrote a measurement test that
asserted nothing - sixteen printed rows with no way to fail - noticed, deleted it, and folded its
one real conclusion into a test that can fail. And it held Q2 at a different moment (19:30, sun
13.3 degrees up) than the sheet's standard 13:00, because at midday the sun sits 61.7 degrees up and
no level frame contains it, so a tight-vs-soft comparison there would have shown two identical
skies. Held constant within the question, and stated on the sheet.
